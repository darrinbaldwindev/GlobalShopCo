# GlobalShopCo Catalogue Blueprint v1

## Purpose

Define the commercial rules for the GlobalShopCo master Shopify catalogue and its future headless WordPress verticals.

## Canonical commerce model

Shopify is the single source of truth for products, variants, pricing, inventory, carts, checkout and orders.

WordPress/headless sites are presentation and merchandising layers. They must not create duplicate product, inventory, pricing or checkout systems.

A Shopify product may be surfaced by multiple specialist WordPress verticals.

## Product selection standard

A product should enter the master catalogue only when there is a defensible case for:

1. Demand — evidence of meaningful consumer demand or strong category relevance.
2. Supplier reliability — identifiable supplier with credible fulfilment capability.
3. Australian suitability — availability, shipping and applicable product requirements can be verified.
4. Free-delivery viability — product economics can support the GlobalShopCo delivery proposition at the intended basket/price model.
5. Margin — sufficient gross margin remains after product and fulfilment costs and applicable transaction costs.
6. Competition — there is a credible value proposition against major marketplaces and retailers.
7. Operational simplicity — preference for products with manageable returns, low breakage and low support burden.
8. Vertical fit — product can contribute to one or more planned specialist storefronts.

## Product lifecycle

RESEARCH → VERIFIED → ECONOMICALLY VIABLE → SHOPIFY DRAFT → READY FOR PUBLICATION → ACTIVE

Products that fail verification or economics remain DRAFT or are rejected. Unverified safety, regulatory, medical, electrical, connected-device or performance claims must not be published as established facts.

## Initial catalogue strategy

Build a curated core catalogue before pursuing catalogue scale. Initial target is approximately 50 high-quality products across the strongest non-regulated/high-friction categories, followed by expansion based on measured performance and supplier confidence.

The first catalogue should favour categories with substantial Australian online demand while excluding or deferring areas where compliance, fulfilment or delivery economics make the first launch unnecessarily complex.

Indicative priority categories:

- Home & Garden
- Fashion & Apparel
- Consumer Electronics & Accessories
- Health & Beauty
- Hobbies & Recreation
- Kids & Baby
- Pet
- Kitchen & Dining
- Home Organisation
- Outdoor

Food/liquor and other restricted or high-compliance categories require separate assessment before inclusion.

## Specialist WordPress verticals

Initial candidate verticals include:

- Baby
- Pet
- Safety
- Home
- Beauty
- Outdoor
- Personal Care
- Home Organisation
- Travel

These are merchandising/site concepts, not independent commerce databases. The same Shopify product can appear on more than one vertical when appropriate.

## Free-delivery rule

Free delivery is a commercial constraint, not merely a marketing label. Product selection must model the expected fulfilment/delivery cost and preserve acceptable contribution margin at the intended basket value.

Low-price products should generally be assessed for bundle/basket-building potential rather than treated as isolated free-delivery transactions.

## First vertical slice relationship

The catalogue work runs in parallel with the technical MVP:

Shopify test/verified product → Storefront API → WordPress/headless presentation → Shopify cart → Shopify checkout.

The first technical slice does not require the full catalogue. Conversely, catalogue research does not require the WordPress implementation to be complete.

## Current seed catalogue

Existing Shopify Baby/Pet/Safety draft products are treated as research candidates rather than approved launch products. Their current draft status, zero inventory and incomplete pricing/supplier/compliance information mean they should not be published without verification.

## Governance

No product should be published solely to demonstrate activity. Commercial evidence and data quality take precedence over catalogue size.

This blueprint should be revised when supplier research, pricing economics, customer evidence or the first headless vertical slice provides stronger evidence.
