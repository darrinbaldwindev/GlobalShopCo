# AU Wooden Toy Compliance Gate — 2026-09-03

**Status:** PRE-LAUNCH COMPLIANCE GATE / NOT APPROVED  
**Scope:** Eleganter Australia children's wooden-toy candidate SKUs and any future AU wooden-toy catalogue.

## Why this gate exists

Australian suppliers must comply with relevant mandatory product-safety standards before supplying products in Australia, and the ACCC states that suppliers are legally responsible for product safety. This register is an evidence gate only; it is not legal advice or a product approval. [ACCC — Product safety standards and how to comply](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/business/understand-product-safety-rules/product-safety-standards-and-how-to-comply)

## Current applicable screening

For toys designed, labelled or marketed for children up to and including 36 months, the Consumer Goods (Toys for Children up to and including 36 Months of Age) Safety Standard 2023 applies. It covers design, construction and testing and is specifically intended to reduce risks from small parts. The ACCC lists blocks, stacking toys, games/puzzles, push/pull toys and toys with pompoms among examples of toys in scope. [ACCC — Toys up to and including 36 months](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/business/search-mandatory-standards/toys-for-children-up-to-and-including-36-months-of-age-mandatory-standard)

For toys designed or intended for children up to 6 years, the mandatory standard concerning lead and other elements may apply and requires testing for specified migratable elements. [ACCC — Toys containing lead and other elements](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/business/search-mandatory-standards/toys-containing-lead-and-other-elements-mandatory-standard)

If a candidate contains magnets, the toys-containing-magnets mandatory standard and the small high-powered magnets ban must be screened. [ACCC — Toys containing magnets](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/business/search-mandatory-standards/toys-containing-magnets-mandatory-standard) [ACCC — Small high-powered magnets ban](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/business/find-banned-products/small-high-powered-magnets-ban)

If a candidate contains button/coin batteries, the applicable button/coin battery safety and information standards must be screened in addition to the toy rules. Recent ACCC enforcement demonstrates that missing battery warnings can create significant compliance exposure. [ACCC — Button batteries guide](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/consumers/handle-chemicals-and-poisons-safely/button-batteries-guide)

## Required evidence per SKU

Before publication, purchase or supply approval, record:

1. exact supplier SKU, product identity and barcode;
2. intended/labelled age grade and evidence supporting that age grade;
3. materials/components and any batteries, magnets, cords or detachable parts;
4. applicable mandatory standards and bans determined from the actual product configuration;
5. current supplier/manufacturer compliance declaration or equivalent evidence;
6. relevant test reports/certificates from an appropriately qualified or accredited laboratory where testing is required;
7. required warnings, age labels, packaging and instructions;
8. Australian importer/supplier responsibility and traceability information;
9. recall/adverse-event history check;
10. evidence date and source location.

Supplier marketing claims such as "child-safe" or "non-toxic" are useful supporting signals but are **not** treated as substitutes for SKU-level compliance evidence.

## Relationship to commercial gate

Compliance is independent of economics. A SKU must satisfy both the product-safety gate and the GlobalShopCo commercial gate. Public RRP, supplier-listed price, dropship terms, stock status or estimated freight cannot override a missing compliance record.
