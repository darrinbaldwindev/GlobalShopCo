# GlobalShopCo — Pet E-commerce & Agentic Priority Evidence — 2026-09-04

## Evidence
Australia Post's 2026 eCommerce Report pet category data reports $1.4B spent online on pet products in 2025, up 15.2% YoY, with 1.2m households shopping online for pet products and online representing 37% of total pet-product spend. Average basket was $120.62. The report also says 8% of consumers use AI to research pet products and 13% would allow an AI agent to purchase for them. It recommends structured product data, clear pricing/delivery information, fit-for-pet guidance and reviews. ## Classification

### VERIFIED
- Pet is a substantial Australian online commerce category: $1.4B online spend in 2025.
- Online pet-product spend grew 15.2% year over year.
- 1.2m households purchased pet products online in 2025.
- 37% of total pet-product spend was online.
- The category has measurable but currently limited AI-shopping adoption: 8% use AI for research and 13% would permit an AI agent to purchase.

### INFERENCE / RECOMMENDATION
Pet remains a strong first-vertical candidate for GlobalShopCo marketing validation, particularly for products with straightforward attributes, repeat/replenishment potential and low regulatory complexity. The AI signal supports preparing structured product information, but does not justify assuming agentic traffic or purchases.

### COMMERCIAL IMPLICATION
The report's $120.62 average basket provides a useful benchmark for merchandising and free-delivery threshold experiments, but it is not a GlobalShopCo forecast. Any GlobalShopCo threshold must be tested against actual supplier freight, product cost, transaction fees and contribution margin.

### UNKNOWN
- GlobalShopCo's achievable basket size, conversion rate, CAC and contribution margin.
- Which specific pet SKU families will perform best.
- Supplier-level economics and fulfilment quality.
- Incremental conversion attributable to AI discoverability.

## Required next test
Prioritise 5–10 actual pet SKUs with authenticated supplier cost/freight, stock, delivery promise, returns and customer price. Model contribution at the observed/benchmark basket range and test whether free delivery remains commercially viable.

## Gate
This evidence strengthens the pet vertical hypothesis. It does not approve launch, suppliers, SKUs, pricing or revenue forecasts.
